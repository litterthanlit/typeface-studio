# Fewer, wider steps fix litt.md's hierarchy

litt.md's hierarchy feels unresolved because three structural gaps stack up under its single-weight rule. **Its heading sizes step by only 1.14–1.17× (32 → 28 → 24)**, closer than any surveyed system stacks levels without adding bold. **Its smallest heading (Hanken Grotesk 20px over Inter 17px reading text) is only 7% taller in x-height**, because Hanken's x-height is about 10% lower than Inter's. That is below the closest any regular-weight title gets to body text in Apple, Material or Carbon. And **the system has no rule for space around headings or between paragraphs**, which is the lever single-weight leaders lean on hardest. The companies closest to a 400-only rule (Vercel's marketing site, Cursor, Ramp, Stripe's 2026 homepage) build rank from four things: one or two large size jumps, a two-step ink system with same-size two-tone headlines, two to four times more space above a heading than below it, and 128–330px between sections. Inter works best as a body face at 15–17px, with 1.6–1.65 leading (its tall x-height needs the upper half of the usual range), about −0.006em tracking with the optical-size axis active, and weight 400 in light mode. No production system sets Inter body at 350–370 in light mode. The only defensible use of 370 is dark mode. The proposal, in priority order: cut the headings to three sizes (40 / 30 / 22) with whole-pixel leading; add a spacing ladder (8 / 16 / 24 / 48 / 64 within a page, sections at 96 / 128 / 160); make fg the only heading color and fg-subtle the color for second clauses and metadata; request Inter's opsz axis from Google Fonts explicitly, because a plain `wght@400` request returns a static file with no optical sizing; and shrink `--measure` from 65ch, which holds about 87 Inter characters, to 54ch (about 73). One pairing must be proven before adopting: Hanken 22 over Inter 17.

## Single-weight systems separate levels with 1.33× jumps, not 1.15× steps

No surveyed design system uses a geometric "modular" ratio. Carbon generates its scale from an additive formula (+2px steps up to 20, then +4, then +6) ([Carbon typography overview](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/typography/overview/)). Material 3, Apple, Atlassian, Polaris, Primer, Radix and Tailwind all ship hand-tuned, mostly even-pixel steps. The 32/28/24 run that litt.md uses does exist elsewhere, but never as three levels stacked on one page at one weight. Atlassian sets it at weight 653 ([Atlassian tokens](https://unpkg.com/@atlaskit/tokens@20.6.1/dist/esm/artifacts/tokens-raw/atlassian-typography.js)). Material 3 uses it as large, medium and small alternatives within a single "headline" role ([Material 3 typescale tokens](https://unpkg.com/@material/web@2.3.0/tokens/v0_192/_md-sys-typescale.scss)). Where heading levels genuinely sit together, as in long-form prose, the steps are 1.2–1.5× and bold comes on top. Tailwind's prose styles run 36/24/20 at weights 800/700/600 ([Tailwind Typography](https://unpkg.com/@tailwindcss/typography@0.5.16/src/styles.js)). GitHub's markdown runs 32/24/20, all semibold ([github-markdown-css](https://unpkg.com/github-markdown-css@5.8.1/github-markdown.css)).

Mature systems do carry their *upper* scale at regular weight. Apple's Large Title through Title 3 are all Regular ([Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Material's display and headline styles are 400. Carbon's 20–32px headings are regular and its 42–54px headings light ([Carbon styles.js](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/type@11.40.0/src/styles.js)). Every one of them switches to semibold or medium where a heading approaches body size. Apple's Headline is the same 17pt as Body and differs only by being Semibold. Carbon's 14 and 16px headings are semibold. Material's title-medium and title-small are 500. Carbon states the rule directly: a lighter font outranks a bold one only "if the lighter weight type size is significantly larger" ([Carbon typography overview](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/typography/overview/)). The closest any regular-weight title comes to body text in these systems is **Apple's Title 3 at 1.176× Body**. Below roughly 1.2×, they all add weight. litt.md has ruled out that exit, so its smallest heading has to stand on size, color and space alone.

The two-family split shrinks the size channel further. Hanken Grotesk's x-height is 0.493em. Inter's is 0.546em at the text optical size and 0.541em at 17px ([Inter technical details](https://rsms.me/inter/); [Hanken Grotesk file](https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/hankengrotesk/v12/ieVn2YZDLWuGJpnzaiwFXS9tYtpd59A.woff2)). Set at the same pixel size, Hanken looks about 10% smaller. A px ratio between a Hanken heading and Inter body is therefore not the visual ratio, as the table shows. To reproduce Apple's 1.176 step over Inter 17px, Hanken needs to be **about 22px**. These are my calculations from the measured x-heights.

The rest of litt.md's scale is closer to right. Every system tightens the line-height ratio as size grows, from about 1.5 at 16px to 1.25 at 32px and 1.0–1.1 at display sizes. Most express line heights as whole pixels, usually on a 4px grid ([Material 3 tokens](https://unpkg.com/@material/web@2.3.0/tokens/v0_192/_md-sys-typescale.scss); [Tailwind theme](https://unpkg.com/tailwindcss@4.1.11/theme.css)). Six of litt.md's nine steps resolve to fractions: 36.8, 33.6, 28.05, 23.25, 19.5 and 16.8px. That is not a rendering defect. Linear, Stripe, Raycast and Cursor all ship fractional line boxes ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)). But fractions make spacing arithmetic inexact, and the nearest whole values are already the industry's standard pairs: 15/24 at Linear and in Mercury's product, 13/20 at Polaris and Mercury, and 12/16 almost everywhere ([Polaris text tokens](https://unpkg.com/@shopify/polaris-tokens@9.4.2/dist/cjs/src/themes/base/text.js); [Mercury demo dashboard](https://demo.mercury.com/dashboard)). Tracking is not the problem. The surveyed systems use tracking to compensate for size, not to signal rank. litt.md's −0.022 to −0.025em at 32–40px matches Stripe (−0.02em at 32–48px), Linear (−0.022em) and Cursor (−0.02em at 36px) ([Linear CSS tokens](https://linear.app/)). The evidence pulls in opposite directions here. Mapping Inter's own spacing curve onto Hanken's metrics suggests easing to about −0.012 to −0.017em. Vercel's Geist, which like Hanken has no optical-size axis, runs −0.04 to −0.06em at display sizes ([Geist Typography](https://vercel.com/geist/typography)). That conflict calls for a visual test, not a change.

## Proximity has to do the work bold does elsewhere

The rule for space around headings is explicit and consistent across sources. Butterick: "headings relate to the text that follows, not the text before," so the space below should be smaller than the space above. Where paragraphs already have space between them, "the space you add around a heading should be larger" ([Butterick, Space above and below](https://practicaltypography.com/space-above-and-below.html)). He also calls space "the best way to emphasize a heading", and endorses non-bold headings set apart by space ([Butterick, Headings](https://practicaltypography.com/headings.html)). Production long-form pages follow this, usually with ratios steeper than the prose defaults. The two counterexamples, Vercel's blog at 24/24 and Arc's legacy blog at 48/48, lean on size instead. Vercel's blog sets a 32px h2 over 18px body, a 1.78× jump that litt.md does not have ([Vercel blog](https://vercel.com/blog/vercel-ship-2026-recap); [Arc blog](https://arc.net/blog/school-of-browser)).

All values are box-to-box px.

litt.md has none of this. `foundations.md` defines section rhythm (`--section-y`) and a spacing scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 160). It bans 20, 28, 36 and 40 as off-scale and limits 4 and 12 to the inside of components. It says nothing about headings or paragraphs. The only flow spacing anywhere in the system is local to one component: `components.md` gives the MessageText renderer paragraphs 12px apart and a measure of "about 68ch". Every other page improvises. In a system that has given up bold, that missing ladder is the single largest source of the "not nailed" feeling. The comparators' most common paragraph gap, 20px, is also off litt.md's grid, so values have to be chosen rather than copied.

The choice of values depends on half-leading. All the numbers above are CSS margins between element boxes. The visible gap, from the last baseline above to the next cap top, also includes each line's half-leading and its ascender or descender space. My calculation from the published metrics puts that at about **7.8px on each side of an Inter 17/28 line and 6px for a Hanken 22/28 line** (Inter: ascender 1984, descender 494 and cap 1490 on a 2048 unit-per-em grid, per [rsms.me/inter](https://rsms.me/inter/); Hanken: 1000/−303/697 per 1000 units). The consequence is not obvious. With 16px between paragraphs, a 16px margin under a heading shows 30.5px against the paragraphs' 31.6px, so the heading binds no tighter to its own text than one paragraph binds to the next. A 24px paragraph gap fixes this and makes every relationship distinct: 22px visible under a minor heading, 31px under a section heading, 40px between paragraphs, 62px above a minor heading and 79px above a section heading. CSS `text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic` would make margins equal visible gaps. It is now supported in Chrome 133, Safari 18.2 and, per MDN's compatibility data, Firefox 154 ([MDN browser-compat-data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/text-box-trim.json); [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-text-box-trim)). litt.md already trims control labels with `.litt-trim`. For running text, though, staying untrimmed is the better call: every comparator number was measured untrimmed, so they transfer directly. Trimming earns its keep in one specific place: aligning cap tops across the 3/9 split, which Linear achieves by setting its section h2 at line-height 1.0 ([linear.app/plan](https://linear.app/plan)).

Section spacing is where litt.md's restraint and its spacing pull against each other. litt.md applies `--section-y` once, as padding-top, so the gap between section contents is 64 / 96 / 128px by breakpoint. Linear pads sections 128px on each side, and the measured gaps between contents come to 256–330px ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)). Vercel uses 160px, rising to 208px from its medium breakpoint ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com/)). Raycast uses 224px ([raycast.com](https://www.raycast.com/)). Stripe and Resend pad about 96px per side, roughly 192px between contents ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com/); [resend.com](https://resend.com/)). Ramp's large spacer is 80 / 96 / 128px by breakpoint. Cursor's sections are 134.4px apart. So litt.md's desktop value ties the bottom of the range and its 64px mobile value falls below it. A system that caps headlines at 40px *so that* space can supply presence should not sit at the floor of section spacing. Hero stacks have no design-system documentation at all, only brand measurements. Headline to subhead runs 8–16px at Resend, Notion, Mercury, Raycast and Linear's brand page (24px at Ramp and Vercel). Subhead to call-to-action runs 20–48px, with a median in the mid-30s ([Raycast AI page](https://www.raycast.com/core-features/ai); [notion.com](https://www.notion.com/); [linear.app/brand](https://linear.app/brand)).

## Two ink steps and same-size two-tone lines carry emphasis without weight

Linear's text colors are four tokens on #08090a: primary #f7f8f8, secondary #d0d6e0, tertiary #8a8f98 and quaternary #62666d. By my calculation their contrast is 18.7:1, 13.6:1, 6.1:1 and 3.5:1. Primary sets headings, inline links and strong text. Secondary sets article and docs body. Tertiary sets hero subheads, bylines, dates and the dim half of two-tone headlines. Quaternary is reserved for legal links and labels ([linear.app](https://linear.app/); [Linear Docs](https://linear.app/docs/inbox)). **litt.md's dark ramp is effectively identical**: fg #f7f8f9, fg-muted #d0d6e0, fg-subtle #8a8f98 and fg-disabled #62666d, on the same #08090a canvas (`tokens.css`). Its dark-mode color hierarchy is already proven in production. Vercel runs the same structure in light mode. gray-1000 and gray-900 (17.2:1 and 8.1:1) carry nearly all text, while gray-800 and gray-700 (3.9:1 and 3.1:1) are kept for metadata and step numbers ([Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)). Linear's 2026 refresh states the principle behind it: "not every element of the interface should carry equal visual weight", which it achieves by dimming rather than bolding ([Linear, A calmer interface](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)).

Two devices from this research transfer directly to a 400-only system. The first is the **two-tone headline at constant size and weight**. Linear sets a 48px h2 in tertiary with its first sentence in primary at the same weight ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)). Stripe follows a 32px #061b31 H2 with a same-size clause in #64748d. Ramp wraps the second clause in a 60%-alpha `text-hushed` span (5.24:1) ([Ramp CSS bundle](https://ramp.com/_next/static/immutable/chunks/2s7hjk6iwxrkd.css)). The second is the **same-size lockup**. Raycast pairs a 20px white heading with a 20px #6A6B6C description ([raycast.com](https://www.raycast.com/)). Cursor sets 22px fg over 22px at 60% ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com/)). Linear's feature titles sit 4px above their descriptions at the same 15/24, though Linear also steps the title to weight 510, a cue litt.md has to replace with color ([linear.app/plan](https://linear.app/plan)). litt.md already holds the seed of this. Its foundations say a same-size label should be fg "and its neighbours fg-subtle", and MessageText sets strong text "in `--fg` at 400". But `tokens.css` resets `b` and `strong` to weight 400 and gives them no color, **so emphasis in running text vanishes everywhere outside MessageText**.

The constraints are clear. WCAG 2.2 requires 4.5:1 below "large scale". At regular weight, large scale starts at 24px (18pt); the 14pt-bold route is closed to a 400-only system ([W3C Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). litt.md's ramp passes everywhere, by my calculation. In light mode, fg is 18.5:1 on canvas, fg-muted 10.2:1 and fg-subtle 5.4:1 (4.8:1 on surface-3). In dark mode they are 18.7, 13.6 and 6.1:1 (5.2:1 on surface-3). Primer cautions against using color "as a primary method of emphasis" and dims only GitHub's lowest heading ([Primer typography](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/typography/)). Carbon says to keep running text neutral. Tailwind's prose sets headings one gray step darker than body (900 against 700) ([Tailwind Typography](https://unpkg.com/@tailwindcss/typography@0.5.16/src/styles.js)). Taken together, color should separate headings from body and one clause from another, not rank heading levels against each other. Three readable steps plus a disabled step matches Linear's and Vercel's four, so litt.md needs no new color.

## Inter wants full weight, generous leading and a requested opsz axis

Inter's designer published size-specific settings as "Dynamic Metrics". The formula is tracking = −0.0223 + 0.185 × e^(−0.1745 × size), with line height = round(1.4 × size). That gives **−0.0088em at 15px and −0.0128em at 17px** ([Inter Dynamic Metrics, archived](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/)). The page was written for Inter 3 and now returns a 404. Inter 4 adds an optical-size axis running from 14 to 32. Browsers set it automatically from the CSS px size (`font-optical-sizing: auto` is the default), and its tighter spacing already supplies part of that tracking ([MDN font-optical-sizing](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-optical-sizing); [Inter 4.0 release](https://github.com/rsms/inter/releases/tag/v4.0)). Corrected for this, litt.md's reading tracking (−0.006em at 17px) sits inside the equivalent band of −0.005 to −0.008em. Its body tracking (−0.003em at 15px) is about 0.003em looser than its band of −0.006 to −0.007em. Production systems agree. Linear tracks 15px marketing text at −0.011em and its in-app editor at −0.0067em ([Linear CSS tokens](https://linear.app/)), and GoCardless uses −1% at 14–16px ([GoCardless typography](https://brand.gocardless.com/typography)). The positive body tracking seen at Cursor, Stripe 2020 and Raycast comes from other typefaces, or from Inter on a dark background. Inter's wide text-size sidebearings are exactly why its own curve turns negative above 12px. On leading, Inter's tall x-height means 1.55 on Inter gives the same interline space, measured in x-heights, as 1.40 on Hanken. That favors the upper half of the conventional 1.4–1.6 range, which is where Linear (1.6) and GoCardless (1.6) set reading text.

The lighter weights under consideration have weaker support than they first appear to. By measured ink, Inter 400 is 6.6–7.9% darker than Hanken 400, and Inter at 365–370 matches it. Hanken 400 itself falls between Inter's 360 and 370 ([Inter 4.1 font file](https://rsms.me/inter/font-files/InterVariable.woff2?v=4.1)). At 370, lowercase stems are about 1.06, 1.22 and 1.38 CSS px at 13, 15 and 17px. Inter's variable and web fonts ship without hinting, which makes thin stems a real risk on 1× Windows displays ([Inter help.txt](https://momjian.us/main/font/inter/help.txt)). **No production system was found that sets Inter body below 400 in light mode**, and GoCardless forbids it outright. The light-body brands, Stripe (Söhne 300), Ramp's product (Lausanne 300) and Mercury (Arcadia 360), all use other typefaces. Stripe and Mercury go back to 400 in dense product UI ([Stripe Dashboard tokens](https://dashboard.stripe.com/login); [Mercury demo dashboard](https://demo.mercury.com/dashboard)). A 459-person Google study found light mode read faster and found a benefit from heavier grades in light mode ([Readability Matters](https://readabilitymatters.org/articles/research-highlight-how-bold-can-we-be)). Dark mode is the exception. Light text on dark looks heavier, and CSS-Tricks thins body text from 400 to 350 there so that "the two variants now look a lot more balanced" ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=306566)). Inter has no grade axis, so weight is the only available lever, and 370 changes line length by just 0.4%. Matching Hanken's ink is also the wrong goal. Headings are larger and set in fg, and the luminance gap between fg (18.5:1) and fg-muted (10.2:1) is far bigger than a 7% difference in ink.

Delivery decides which settings are real. Google Fonts serves Inter with its opsz and weight axes but strips every character variant and stylistic set. Its file contains only calt, ccmp, dnom, frac, locl, numr, pnum and tnum ([Google Fonts Inter file](https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/inter/v20/UcCo3FwrK3iLTcviYwY.woff2)). Raycast and Notion both declare features their served files cannot honor ([raycast.com](https://www.raycast.com/); [notion.com](https://www.notion.com/)). My tests today found that **requesting `Inter:wght@400` returns a static file, and `Inter:wght@350..400` returns a weight-only variable file. Only an explicit `opsz` request delivers the optical-size axis** ([Google Fonts CSS API](https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Inter:opsz,wght@14..32,370..400)). The tracking values above assume that axis is present. Without it, Inter renders its text design at every size and needs the literal Dynamic Metrics values (−0.009 and −0.013em). `line-height: normal` must never be used across the pair, because it resolves to 1.21em in Inter and 1.303em in Hanken. Hanken's Google Fonts build also lacks tabular figures ([Hanken Grotesk file](https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/hankengrotesk/v12/ieVn2YZDLWuGJpnzaiwFXS9tYtpd59A.woff2)). Live numbers at heading sizes therefore belong in Inter with `tnum`, as Stripe and Mercury set money at −0.03em with tabular figures ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com/); [Mercury demo dashboard](https://demo.mercury.com/dashboard)), or in Geist Mono.

The measure token does not do what its name says. The `ch` unit is the width of the font's zero. My measurement of Inter 4.1 puts that zero at 0.628em at 17px, against an average English character, spaces included, of 0.467em. **So 1ch holds about 1.35 Inter characters, and `--measure: 65ch` comes to about 694px, or roughly 87 characters at 17px.** Today, with Hanken, the same token holds about 82 characters (zero 0.560em, average character 0.446em). After the switch to Inter, the 640px `--container-reading` becomes the real cap at about 81 characters, and MessageText's 68ch reaches about 91. The guidance runs from 45 to 90 characters (Butterick), at most 80 (WCAG 1.4.8 AAA, Primer) and 65–75 (GoCardless) ([Butterick, Line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html); [W3C Understanding 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)). The brands' 76–93-character blog columns are a pattern in the data, not evidence that long lines read better. As a pairing, Hanken over Inter is low in contrast, which Butterick says "can be more effective than higher contrast" ([Butterick, Mixing fonts](https://practicaltypography.com/mixing-fonts.html)). Hanken's proportions nearly match Inter Display's: an x-to-cap ratio of 0.707 against 0.709, and the same average glyph width. Structurally this is Linear's 2024 move, a separate display design for headings with regular Inter for everything else ([Linear redesign, part II](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). No published system pairs Hanken with Inter, so the pairing itself stays unproven.

## The proposal: three heading sizes, one spacing ladder, explicit ink roles

### Priority 1: cut the headings to 40, 30 and 22, with whole-pixel leading

The scale drops from nine steps to seven. It keeps 40px as the ceiling and puts every co-occurring heading level at least 1.33× above the next. The smallest heading is set at the 22px floor that reproduces Apple's regular-weight Title 3 step over Inter 17. All line heights are whole px on a 4px grid. Heading tracking stays on litt.md's existing size curve, interpolated for the new sizes. Display and h1 share metrics: display stays the name for a marketing hero, and h1 is the page title on documents, so step names follow HTML levels on content pages.

The sizes map onto litt.md's three surfaces like this. On marketing pages, 40 is the hero statement, 30 the one statement per section, and 22 the card, feature and left-column section labels (the old h4's role), with body 15 and reading 17 for subheads. On documents and long-form pages, 40 is the page title, 30 the sections and 22 the subsections, over reading 17: three levels, Butterick's limit. In app UI, 30 is the page title and 22 the panel, group and dialog title, over body 15, where 22 clears body by 1.33× in x-height. Below 22, rank comes from same-size lockups: a title in fg at 15 or 17 over a description in fg-subtle, 4px apart, as Linear, Raycast and Cursor do. If testing shows 22 is still too weak over reading text, the fallback is 40 / 32 / 24. That keeps the 1.33× jump between the two in-text levels and accepts 1.25× between display and h1, which never share a page.

### Priority 4: set Inter for the delivery litt.md actually has

Load `family=Hanken+Grotesk:wght@400&family=Inter:opsz,wght@14..32,370..400&family=Geist+Mono:wght@400`. I confirmed today that this request resolves, with Inter declared at `font-weight: 370 400`. Leave `font-optical-sizing` at its `auto` default, and never set a global `font-variation-settings` that pins opsz. Set reading and body at 400 in light mode and 370 in dark mode; small and caption stay at 400 in both themes. Declare no `cv` or `ss` features, because they do nothing in the Google Fonts build. Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for changing numbers set in Inter, and keep Geist Mono for tables and data as litt.md already does. Set `--measure: 54ch` on Inter elements themselves rather than on a Hanken wrapper, because `ch` resolves against the element's own font. That gives about 576px and 73 characters at 17px, and about 510px at 15px. Self-hosting the rsms 4.1 files becomes worth it only if litt.md later wants Linear's cv01/ss03 alternates or hinted static fonts for 1× Windows. The hinted static fonts lose the opsz axis. Update the foundations text "Hanken Grotesk carries everything" and "No third family, ever" to match the decided split.

### Priority 5: give sections the space the 40px cap assumes

Move `--section-y` from 64 / 96 / 128 to **96 / 128 / 160** at the same breakpoints, still applied once as padding-top. Both values are on the existing scale. The mobile step keeps the section gap above the 64px h2 gap, so the ladder stays in order at every width. The desktop step moves litt.md from the floor of the comparator range (Ramp's 128) toward its middle (about 192 at Stripe and Resend). It stays well short of Linear's 256+, which suits the system's restraint.

## Conclusion

A system that gives up bold and caps its headlines at 40px moves its hierarchy budget out of type and into space and ink. Its spacing rules therefore need to be at least as precise as its type scale. litt.md has it the other way round: nine type steps specified to three decimal places of an em, and no rule at all for the space above a heading. The scale problem is also font-relative. Once headings and body come from two families, a pixel ratio stops being a visual ratio, so every heading step should be checked in x-heights, where Hanken 20 over Inter 17 is barely a step. The switch to Inter also quietly changes the meaning of tokens nobody plans to touch: `65ch` turns into about 87 characters, and optical sizing depends on a Google Fonts URL parameter.

The evidence leaves three things genuinely open. No study gives a minimum perceptible ratio between heading levels, so the 1.33× rule and the 22px floor are reasoned from conventions in mature systems, not measured on readers. Hanken has no published tracking guidance, so display tracking is a matter of taste to settle by eye. And thin-stem rendering on 1× Windows has not been tested at all, which is why the 370 dark-mode weight is a candidate rather than a decision. These three tests should run first; the rest of the proposal is low-risk and can ship with them.
